# energy/snowpack-ripening

## What it asserts

A pack below freezing does not drain. Water produced at its surface percolates
into colder snow and refreezes, and the latent heat released pays down the
pack's cold content. Water leaves only once that deficit is gone and the pore
space has filled. That is ripening (DeWalle and Rango, 2008), and it is how
Snow-17 routes its outflow (Anderson, 2006): water reaching a pack that still
carries `NEGHS` goes back into the ice, and only the excess over the deficit
and the liquid-holding capacity leaves.

That is the bulk, or matrix-flow, picture of a snowpack, and the probe is a check
of that picture and not of every way a model may move water through snow. A
model that represents preferential flow is outside it; see *Applicability*.

The probe holds the water a model reports leaving its snow module, `snm`, to
the cold content the same model reports, `csnow`:

```
R   = sum over block steps that END with csnow_t > C_eps  of  snm_t * dt
eps = R / W_peak  <=  0.05                     W_peak the peak `snw`
```

A failure means water left a pack that the model itself says had not ripened.

### Why outflow, not melt

The ROADMAP entry this replaces, `energy/snowpack-cold-content`, asked that
**melt** not occur while the pack is below freezing. That rule would fail a
correct layered model. In a real pack, surface melt percolates into colder snow
beneath it and refreezes there (Marsh and Woo, 1984), so melt and a positive
bulk cold content coexist every spring morning. Refreezing inside the pack
releases no water, so it is invisible here. Only water that leaves is counted.

### Why the end of the step

A step counts when the pack still holds cold content **at its end**: the water
left, and the pack was cold afterwards, so it cannot have left because the pack
ripened. A step that ends ripe may have paid its deficit and drained within
itself. Snow-17 does exactly that when the water reaching it outweighs `NEGHS`,
and counting that step would fail a model for ripening on the day it ripened.

## The case

The case is `energy/snowmelt-energy-water`'s, generated by the same body
(`generate()` is byte-identical): one snow year at `PT1D`, 30 days of spinup,
150 of accumulation below −12 °C, then a 60-day dry block that opens below
−14 °C and ends above +4 °C. Only the block, labelled `melt` in `_regime`, is
scored. It has to be dry, because `snm` counts rain passing through the snow
module, and the criterion refuses a block that is not.

`requires` names `snm`, `snw` and `csnow`, and **no forcing**. The criterion
reads `rn` and `tas` from the case as bounds on what the weather makes
possible, not as drivers a model must consume. In this suite, requiring a
forcing means "consume it or be N/A". Requiring `rn` would make exactly the
temperature-index models this probe exists to reach N/A, since Snow-17 declares
`needs_forcing: [pr, tas, pet]`.

## Applicability

Eligibility here is set by what a model reports (`snm`, `snw`, `csnow`) and not
by how it transports water. A model that represents preferential flow, water
moving through flow fingers before the bulk of the pack has ripened (Wever et
al., 2016), can drain a pack whose bulk cold content is positive by design, and
would read here as drainage from a pack that had not ripened. That is a
difference in the process assumed, not a violation of conservation.

No model that reports `snm` and `csnow` represents preferential flow today:
Snow-17, the energy-balance reference and the two broken references all route
matrix flow. If one is submitted, whether it is scored here is a maintainer
decision to take on its proposal issue before its rows are archived, because
the harness would otherwise record the exceedance as FAIL (VIOLATION). The
probe adds no declaration for it. A flag a model sets for itself is a way out
of the check, and the decision should be what sets it.

## Tolerance

**5 percent of the peak pack.** Not zero, because melt at the snow-ground
interface forms at the base, never crosses the cold pack, and drains however
cold the pack is. Snow-17 does exactly that (`DAYGM`, 0.1 mm/day in its
adapter), and every millimetre it releases while cold is ground melt. Over
seeds 0–199 that is 0.39 to 0.68 % of the peak pack, which the rule clears 7.4
times over.

**300 mm peak pack floor.** Ground melt is fixed per day, not proportional to
the pack, so the share it makes depends on the pack's size. NWS documents
`DAYGM` from 0.0 mm/day, where the ground freezes, to 0.3 mm/day in mild
climates with deep snow. At 0.3 mm/day over the longest cold stretch this case
produces, 47 days, a pack releases 14.1 mm while cold, which is inside 5 %
only above 282 mm. A thinner pack is refused as N/A rather than scored, unless
more left it while cold than 0.3 mm/day over its cold days could explain: then
the failure is measured, not inferred, and it stands. A refusal may cost a pass
but never buys one. This
case's smallest peak over seeds 0–199 is 621.6 mm, so at its own sizes the rule
covers `DAYGM` up to 0.66 mm/day, over twice the documented range.

## Guards

The residual rests on the model's own cold content, so the criterion also
bounds what that report can say. Each check is set by this case, not by
snowpacks in general.

- **Non-negative.** A negative `csnow` takes its step out of the sum, and a
  negative `snm` cancels drainage inside it.
- **A reported liquid column has to be a number inside the pack.** `lwsnl` is
  optional, so nothing upstream validates it, and it sets the ice the pack's
  temperature is read through. Reported, it must be finite on every row and
  between 0 and `snw` (within `liquid_tolerance_mm`, 10^-6 mm). Absent still
  means no liquid. Without this, one blank cell made the supply bound below
  select a NaN and skip itself, and a write-off that fails as `ripens_too_fast`
  passed. The bound's own result is also checked for NaN, so a bad value in the
  case's forcing cannot do the same.
- **The pack must open as ice.** On the row before the block, `snw - lwsnl`
  must be at least half the peak pack. Nothing melts before the block, and the
  honest packs open with 99.99 to 100 % of their peak as ice and no liquid.
  Declaring the pack liquid, or dipping `snw`, on that one row would otherwise
  let a token cold content over a token ice read as a cold pack, or at zero
  ice skip the next check outright.
- **The pack must open cold.** Its mean temperature on the row before the block,
  `-csnow / (c_ice * ice)` with `ice = snw - lwsnl`, must be at least 2 K below
  freezing. The air never rises above −12 °C before the block, and the honest packs
  open at −15.9 to −24.0 °C. Reporting the pack ripe is the plainest way to
  empty the sum, and this is what catches it.
- **Cold content cannot fall faster than energy arrives.** Per step, the fall in
  `csnow` must not exceed net radiation plus sensible heat from air warmer than
  the pack, `[max(0, rn) + K * max(0, tas - T_pack)] * dt`, with the pack's
  temperature taken from its own cold content and ice and floored at the
  coldest air in the record less 2 K. The honest packs never go below that air.
  The floor stops one row with the pack declared almost all liquid from reading
  as impossibly cold and buying unlimited sensible heat. No fall is credited to
  ice leaving: ice that melts is at 0 °C and carries no cold content, and a
  credit for a fall in *reported* ice would let one edited row write the whole
  deficit off. `K = 10 W m-2 K-1` is what a bulk coefficient `rho c_p C_H U` gives
  at about 4 m/s. Snow-17 warms from the air rather than from radiation and
  needs at most 4.28 over every warming step of seeds 0–39. A bound on
  radiation alone would fail it on 111 of 1459 steps. A model that reports its
  true cold content on the opening row and none on the next needs 5.2 to 73
  times the bound. That is what the bound stops, and only that: a **single
  step's** fall. A gradual write-down is the limit in *Scope*, item 1.

## What it catches

On **gate seed 1552450170**:

| Model | Peak pack | Left while cold | Share | Opens at | Verdict |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `reference_snow_energy` | 947.5 mm | 0.0 mm | 0.00 % | −20.3 °C | PASS |
| `sacsma_snow17` | 932.5 mm | 4.4 mm | 0.47 % | −18.1 °C | PASS |
| `reference_degree_day` | 947.5 mm | **359.0 mm** | **37.9 %** | −16.9 °C | FAIL |
| `reference_always_ripe` | — | — | — | **0.0 °C** | FAIL (`opens_ripe`) |

Over the five gate seeds, `reference_degree_day` drains 37.3 to 46.8 % of its
pack while cold, and Snow-17 drains 0.47 to 0.57 %, all of it ground melt. Over
seeds 0–199 the five models land on the declared side in all 1000 runs:
`reference_degree_day` at 27.2 to 77.6 %, Snow-17 at 0.39 to 0.68 %, the
energy-balance pack at 0 %, and `reference_always_ripe` refused as `opens_ripe`
on every seed.

`reference_degree_day` melts on air temperature whatever its cold content, so
water leaves on the last 17 to 23 of the block's 60 steps while its own `csnow`
says the pack is still well below freezing. This is the construction the probe exists to
catch. `reference_always_ripe` is the same pack reporting `csnow = 0`
throughout, so the sum has nothing to count. It is caught because it opens the
block ripe after a winter that never reached freezing.

`reference_warming_free` also passes. Its water side is `reference_snow_energy`'s
and only its energy accounting differs, which `energy/snowmelt-energy-water`
catches and this probe, reading no energy fluxes, is not meant to.

### Baselines

`must_pass` names `reference_snow_energy` and `sacsma_snow17`. Snow-17 reports
no energy fluxes, so it is N/A on `energy/snowmelt-energy-water`; it is scored
here because it carries its cold content directly as `NEGHS`, reported as
`NEGHS × λ_f`. Reaching temperature-index models is the point of the probe.

## Scope

What this probe does **not** test:

1. **A model that reports less cold content than it has.** The check is a
   consistency check between a model's outflow and its own cold content, not an
   energy balance. No bound built from the forcing alone could close this: over
   this case's winter the pack could absorb 127 to 276 MJ m-2 of radiant energy
   against 24 to 41 MJ m-2 of cold brought in by snowfall, so the forcing admits
   almost any cold content. Only a surface energy balance pins it, which
   `energy/snowmelt-energy-water` carries for models that report one. The
   supply bound does not change this. It stops a cold content dropped in one
   step, not one written down gradually: `reference_degree_day`'s cold content,
   written down at 99 % of the bound's own rate from the opening row, reaches
   zero on block day 12 to 24, and the pack first drains on day 38 to 44 (seeds 0–199). So
   in this case two to four weeks of write-down fit inside the bound, and
   that construction passes. `test_a_cold_content_written_down_inside_the_energy_supply_still_passes`
   keeps it as a reproducible example, so the limit cannot drift into a claim.
2. **Preferential flow.** Real packs can release water through flow fingers
   before the bulk has ripened (Wever et al., 2016). A model that represents
   this will report outflow while cold, and is outside the bulk assumption this
   probe checks; *Applicability* says how that is handled. The 5 % allowance
   absorbs a little of it, and a model that does much more would need a probe
   that scores it against observations rather than against its own bulk cold
   content.
3. **Rain-on-snow.** The block must be dry, for the reason above.
4. **Timing within the step.** A step that ends ripe is excused whole, because
   it may have paid its deficit and drained within itself. Daily, that is the
   whole ripening day; hourly, only its last hour. The blind interval is one
   step's outflow, and a test keeps it visible: 30 mm/day on the ripening day
   is 0 % of the peak pack at a daily step and 5.8 % at an hourly one. Away from
   the transition the two agree.
